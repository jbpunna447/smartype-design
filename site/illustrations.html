Illustrations

Three directions for the art in onboarding, Premium and empty states. Each is drawn here as the Welcome illustration, animated, on light and dark.

Why a new set

Today's four illustrations are 3D clay renders of a graphite key with a puffy star: they match neither the new white key nor the brand spark, and they're images, not vectors. A first flat-vector try with the brand's shapes was clean but read as large icons, with no story and no life. Strong onboarding art (Duolingo, Headspace, Google's Material 3 Expressive) gets its life from a story, a character or motion.

Three concepts

A · Moments

Each illustration is a tiny before → after scene from the product: a typo fixed by the spark, a voice note turning into words, a message flipping into Hindi. The life comes from something happening, and it teaches what SmarType does while it delights.

Works

  • Explains the product at a glance; doubles as a hint on what to tap
  • Every scene reuses real UI (bubbles, smartbar, keys), so it stays true to the app
  • Loops of 3–5 s work as Lottie files in the app and as video in the store

Watch out

  • Needs care to stay simple: one change per scene
  • Text inside art must be localised or kept to universal content

B · The key, alive

The brand key becomes a small character: it hops, blinks and gets an idea (the spark). Each screen gives it a pose: waving hello, holding a note, searching with a magnifier, asleep on an empty list.

Works

  • Most emotion and personality; memorable and easy to recognise
  • One character carries the whole set, store art and website
  • Empty states and errors feel friendly instead of blank

Watch out

  • Can feel childish if overdone: keep the face to two eyes and a smile
  • Competes a little with the logo's key; the character has no S

C · Expressive playground

Bold, springy compositions in the spirit of Google's Material 3 Expressive: a big scalloped shape in the brand gradient, keys tumbling, words in English, Hindi, Spanish and Tamil orbiting, the spark popping.

Works

  • Feels native to modern Android; energetic and premium
  • Shows multilingual typing without words of explanation
  • Easy to make a set: same parts, new arrangement

Watch out

  • More abstract: says less about what each screen does
  • Lots of motion; needs a calm still frame for reduced motion
